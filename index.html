Senior Software Engineer

Jaggaer

Aug 2025 — Present Hyderabad

Building Catelyze, the platform layer that lets several acquired procurement products behave like one. Seven business modules now talk to Catelyze instead of to each other.

Event-Driven Data Sync

The pipeline that moves business objects between modules. An application layer over RabbitMQ, and the only service in the platform with no database of its own.

  • Inbound is one exchange with a single queue bound to it. Outbound is one exchange per business object, with routing keys taken from tenant configuration. The dumb side and the rich side, on purpose.
  • Every exchange, queue, binding and routing key is declared at application startup, so onboarding a new business object is a configuration change and not an infrastructure ticket.
  • Consume, look up config, gate, transform, publish, and only then acknowledge. At-least-once, with redelivery on nack.
  • The pipeline holds no business keys. The consuming modules dedupe, because the business meaning belongs to them.
  • Peaks above 10K+ events/sec at business hours, measured at inbound.

Configuration Console

The single source of truth for tenant configuration, replacing settings that used to live twice, once inside a product and once in that product's own platform layer.

  • Led the low-level design and built a large share of the APIs.
  • Everyone pulls and caches, nothing is pushed. The cache sits in the shared Redis with a one hour TTL, plus a force-invalidate for when an hour is too long.
  • Mostly GraphQL inside the platform, REST where the outside world dictates it.
  • Lets non-engineering teams change behaviour without a deployment.

Third-Party Integration & On-Call

One service per outside vendor, so an external API can never reach into core business logic.

  • Carbon data, supplier account verification and e-invoicing networks, each isolated behind its own service and its own database.
  • Resilience4j retry, circuit breaker and rate limiter on every outbound call.
  • I take on-call, which is where you find out what a design is really worth. Found the root causes of recurring legacy failures and built guardrails into the new platform so the same failure cannot happen twice.
  • Across 100+ migrated tenants, production incidents went from 30+ a month to 2-3 a month.

Senior Software Engineer

LeadSquared

Mar 2023 — Jul 2025 Bengaluru

Built the communication side of Service CRM, a ticketing and helpdesk product. Notifications going out, support mail coming in, and the AI that helps agents answer.

Notification Platform

Designed and built end to end. Every other module in the product publishes into it over Redis Streams, used here as the message bus and not as a cache.

  • Resolves templates through per-channel factories and dispatches to the channel service by configuration. Email, SMS and WhatsApp.
  • The event carries everything the notification needs. The service never calls back to the publisher for missing data, it fails the request. That is what keeps the cost flat at volume.
  • Persist, then acknowledge, then process. The database is the record, the stream is only a handoff.
  • Peaks above 10K+ notifications/sec.

Inbound Mail & the AI Layer

The business rules for email. Which ticket does this mail belong to, should it reopen one, and what can the agent be helped with.

  • Two spam layers on purpose. The provider's own score first, because it costs nothing, then a cheap AI pass over the content for whatever got through.
  • User-triggered retrieval over the tenant's own documents and their past resolved cases, on AWS Bedrock. Suggestions are shown to the agent and then gone. Nothing is stored as a draft.
  • Thread level vendor affinity, so a reply leaves through the provider the conversation arrived on.
  • Attaches every inbound mail to the right ticket and applies the reopen rules.

The Provider Layer

The only door to the outside. Three email providers, three completely different ways of receiving mail, one internal model.

  • A webhook from one, a queue from another, a push subscription from the third. Normalised so nothing downstream knows which provider a mail came from.
  • Persists the mail to the database and the raw body to object storage before acknowledging the provider, so nothing is lost at the boundary.
  • Dedupes on the email Message-ID. The key comes from the domain, not from us.
  • Peaks above 1000+ emails/sec on large payloads.
  • The dual write between the database and object storage was never solved. I know where it bites and what I would do about it.

Cash Reconciliation

Invoice Presentment

  • Built the configuration console for post-payment correspondence.
  • This is where I learned to read a failure properly instead of just retrying it.
